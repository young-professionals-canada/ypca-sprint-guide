## A website is content, presentation, and behaviour

You do not need to code this site, but understanding its parts helps you give better instructions and review the result.

- **HTML** provides the content and structure: headings, paragraphs, sections, links, and forms.
- **CSS** controls presentation: typography, colour, spacing, and layout.
- **JavaScript** adds behaviour: menus, filters, copy buttons, and other interactions.
- **The browser** combines those parts into the page people see.

An AI website builder can generate all three. You are still responsible for the facts, priorities, privacy choices, and final quality.

## Decide what the website must accomplish

A good early-career website helps a visitor answer four questions quickly:

1. Who is this person?
2. What kind of work are they interested in?
3. What evidence shows what they can do?
4. How can I contact or learn more about them?

Everything else is optional. A clear one-page website is more useful than a large site filled with generic text.

## Treat public and private information differently

Your editing preview is private until you publish it. After publishing, assume anyone can copy, save, or share the content. Do not publish a home address, personal identification, confidential work, private client information, references, or documents containing hidden personal data.

> Your resume is the source of truth, not a command to publish every line. Select what helps the visitor and remove what should remain private.
