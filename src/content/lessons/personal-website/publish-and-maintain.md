## Preview first, then publish

Your editing workspace and preview remain private until you choose Publish. Publishing creates a public version and a URL other people can open. Always test that public URL outside your signed-in session; editor previews can hide permission or link problems.

Start with the builder's provided address. A custom domain can make the site easier to remember, but it is optional. The work and message matter more than the address.

## Domain, hosting, and website are different

- **Domain:** the memorable address people type, such as `yourname.ca`.
- **Hosting:** the service that stores and sends the website files.
- **Website:** the pages, content, design, and behaviour visitors receive.

A domain points visitors to the hosting service through DNS records. Buying a domain does not automatically create a website, and publishing a website does not require buying a domain.

If you connect a domain, keep privacy protection enabled, verify the registrar email, copy DNS records exactly, and allow time for changes to propagate. Record the renewal date so the address is not lost accidentally.

## Maintain evidence, not constant novelty

Review the site every three months and after meaningful changes: a new role, completed project, updated resume, publication, certification, or change in direction. Remove expired links and outdated claims.

You do not need to redesign the site every time. Update the evidence, maintain clarity, and keep the contact path working.

> One accurate, clear page you maintain is better than a complex website you abandon.
