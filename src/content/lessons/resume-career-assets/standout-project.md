## Experience is stronger when people can see the work

Early-career applicants are often asked for experience before they have been given many opportunities to gain it. A focused project can create evidence of your thinking, process, judgment, and follow-through.

The goal is not to build the largest possible project. It is to finish a clear piece of work that helps another person understand what you can do.

## Use four boundaries

A workable brief answers four questions:

1. **Problem:** What specific question or need will you address?
2. **Audience:** Who is the finished work for?
3. **Proof:** What will exist when the project is complete?
4. **Next action:** What can you do in 30 minutes without more planning?

For example, “make a data science project” is too broad. “Create a one-page dashboard showing monthly TTC delay patterns for Toronto commuters using the public delay dataset” has a topic, audience, format, and source.

## Design the smallest credible version

Limit the first version to one question, one audience, one main format, and a small set of source material. Add stretch goals only after the core proof is complete.

Useful projects exist in every field: a policy brief, literature review, onboarding guide, campaign plan, lesson resource, research poster, service blueprint, budget model, process improvement proposal, or community event plan.

> Finished and explainable is more valuable than ambitious and incomplete.
