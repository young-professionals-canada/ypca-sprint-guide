## The base resume is your reliable starting point

A base resume is a polished general version—not a document you send unchanged to every employer. It contains your strongest verified evidence in a stable layout, making future tailoring faster and safer.

Most early-career resumes should make five things easy to find: your name and contact details, education, relevant experience, projects or involvement, and skills. The exact order depends on what best supports your next opportunity.

## Select before you rewrite

Do not try to squeeze your entire Master Profile onto one or two pages. Select evidence using three filters:

1. **Strength:** Does it show a meaningful action, skill, or contribution?
2. **Specificity:** Does it include enough context to be credible?
3. **Usefulness:** Could it matter to the opportunities you want next?

Then rewrite the selected notes into concise bullets. A useful pattern is **action + object + context or result**. For example: “Prepared weekly attendance reports in Excel for a community program serving 80 participants.”

## Make the page easy to scan

Use a simple hierarchy, consistent dates, standard section names, and readable type. Avoid skill bars, headshots, dense paragraphs, excessive columns, and decorative elements that compete with the content.

> The template provides order and formatting. Your Master Profile provides the facts. Neither should invent the other.
