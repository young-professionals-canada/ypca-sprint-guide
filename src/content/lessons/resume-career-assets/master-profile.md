## One reliable source beats many inconsistent drafts

A Master Profile is a private, detailed record of your professional evidence. It is longer than a resume because it preserves everything you may need later: dates, responsibilities, tools, examples, outcomes, keywords, and boundaries.

Your resume will change for different opportunities. Your source of truth should not. When every application begins from the same reviewed profile, you are less likely to invent a detail, lose a strong example, or create conflicting dates.

## What belongs in each experience

Keep raw detail under consistent headings:

- **Context:** organization, team, audience, or problem.
- **Dates:** verified month and year where available.
- **What you did:** specific responsibilities and actions.
- **Tools:** software, methods, languages, equipment, and processes you actually used.
- **Verified keywords:** accurate terms employers may search for.
- **Do not claim:** nearby skills or outcomes that would be inaccurate.

The “Do not claim” field is especially important when using AI. It turns a general instruction like “do not make things up” into a concrete boundary.

## Review AI output like an editor

AI can organize your notes, but it cannot determine whether a statement is true. Compare names, dates, numbers, tools, and responsibilities against your source material. Delete polished wording that quietly changes the meaning.

> A useful Master Profile is not the one that sounds most impressive. It is the one you can confidently defend in an interview.
