## Tailoring is prioritization, not reinvention

Employers describe the same skill in different ways. One posting may say “stakeholder communication,” another “client support,” and another “respond to participant inquiries.” Tailoring helps the reader see the connection between their needs and your real experience.

It does not mean forcing every keyword into the document. The job posting tells you what matters. Your Master Profile decides what you can honestly claim.

## Read the posting in layers

Start with the role title and opening paragraph, then required qualifications, then responsibilities, and finally preferred qualifications. Repeated ideas are usually important. Separate requirements into:

- **Supported:** your evidence clearly demonstrates the requirement.
- **Related:** you have transferable evidence but should use careful wording.
- **Gap:** you do not have evidence and should not claim it.

A gap is not a failure. It may become an interview preparation topic, a future learning goal, or a reason to emphasize another strength.

## Change the order before changing every sentence

Often the best tailoring move is structural: bring the most relevant experience, project, skill, or bullet higher on the page. Rewrite only when the employer's language makes your verified evidence clearer.

> If a recruiter asked, “Tell me exactly when you did this,” you should be able to answer from your Master Profile.
