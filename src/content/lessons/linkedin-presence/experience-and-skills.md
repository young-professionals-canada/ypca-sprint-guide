## LinkedIn can show context your resume leaves out

A resume is selective and space-limited. LinkedIn can explain the organization, audience, project, and skills around an experience while remaining concise.

For each position, verify the formal relationship first. LinkedIn's official guidance says an association with an organization must accurately reflect professional experience such as employment, contract work, an official internship, volunteer experience, or board service. Do not list yourself as working for an organization because you used its product, attended an event, or completed an unrelated course.

## Use contributions instead of copied job descriptions

A job description explains what anyone in the role might do. Your profile should explain what you actually did. Use **action + object + context or result**:

- Coordinated weekly volunteer schedules for a community program serving three neighbourhood locations.
- Cleaned and summarized survey responses in Excel for a student research project on transit access.
- Prepared onboarding guides that helped new club executives understand event and reimbursement processes.

Include numbers only when verified and useful. Ordinary but specific contributions are stronger than dramatic claims you cannot defend.

## Skills need context

LinkedIn allows a large skills list, but volume is not the goal. Prioritize skills relevant to your direction and supported by evidence. Where available, associate a skill with the experience, education, project, or credential that demonstrates it.

Use the platform's standard skill name when it accurately matches your evidence. Avoid adding every suggested keyword or listing a tool you only observed someone else use.

> Your profile should help a reader move from “this person lists the skill” to “I can see where this person used it.”
