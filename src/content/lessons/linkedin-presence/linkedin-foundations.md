## LinkedIn is a professional identity system

Your profile is more than an online resume. It is the identity attached to your connection requests, messages, comments, posts, searches, and applications. A clear profile helps people understand why your activity is relevant and whether they should continue the conversation.

Start with one goal. A profile optimized for a first internship may emphasize education, projects, and learning. A profile for a career changer may need to connect prior experience to a new direction. A profile for community leadership may prioritize programs, volunteering, and partnerships.

## Audit before rewriting

Review the profile from top to bottom. Check your name, photo, headline, location, industry, contact information, About section, experience, education, skills, projects, certifications, volunteering, and activity.

Mark each section as:

- **Keep:** accurate, current, and useful.
- **Improve:** true, but vague or poorly prioritized.
- **Add:** supported by evidence but missing.
- **Remove:** outdated, duplicated, private, or misleading.

Use your resume or Master Profile as the source of truth. LinkedIn allows more space and personality, but it should not contain employers, dates, credentials, responsibilities, or outcomes that conflict with your other career documents.

## Make visibility a deliberate choice

Profile edits become visible as soon as you save them, even when you choose not to notify your network. LinkedIn provides separate controls for public-profile visibility and for sharing some job or education changes with your network.

Decide whether your email, website, location, activity, connections, and job-search preferences should be visible. Do not publish a home address, personal identification, confidential work, references, or private client information.

> Build the profile you are comfortable standing behind when a recruiter, classmate, colleague, or community partner opens it without warning.
