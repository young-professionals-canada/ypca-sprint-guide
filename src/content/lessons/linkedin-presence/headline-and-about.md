## The introduction must work in several places

Your name, photo, headline, and basic profile information appear when you comment, connect, message, apply, or appear in search. The headline therefore needs to make sense outside the full profile.

A useful headline usually combines three ideas:

- **Current identity:** what you do, study, or contribute now.
- **Relevant capability:** the skill, topic, or problem you can support with evidence.
- **Direction:** the work, field, or opportunity you want to move toward.

For example, “Business student | Customer research and Excel reporting | Interested in operations and service improvement” is clearer than “Student seeking opportunities.” Use separators only to improve scanning; the words still need to form a coherent idea.

## Write an About section people can repeat

The About section is different from the headline. It gives you room to connect your experience, motivation, strengths, and direction in first person.

Use a simple structure:

1. **Position:** Who are you, and what kind of problems or work interest you?
2. **Proof:** What experiences, projects, tools, or contributions support that position?
3. **Path:** What are you learning, building, or looking for next?
4. **Invitation:** What kind of conversation or opportunity would be welcome?

Keep paragraphs short. The opening should remain useful even if a visitor reads only the first few lines.

## Prefer evidence to adjectives

Instead of “I am a strategic communicator,” explain that you prepared stakeholder updates, facilitated an event, interviewed research participants, or translated technical information into a guide. Specific actions allow the reader to form their own positive conclusion.

> Write for a real person who may want to introduce you to someone else. Make the profile easy to understand and easy to describe accurately.
