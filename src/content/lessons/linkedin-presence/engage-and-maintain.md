## Visibility begins with useful participation

You do not need to publish every day. A thoughtful comment, relevant question, useful introduction, event follow-up, or clear project update can make your interests and judgment visible.

Choose a few topics that connect to your current direction. Follow relevant people and organizations, read before responding, and add something more useful than “Great post.” You might connect an idea to your experience, ask a focused question, summarize a practical takeaway, or share a respectful alternative.

Remember that comments are connected to your professional identity and may be visible beyond your immediate network. Review tone, facts, confidentiality, and relevance before publishing.

## Share evidence while it is fresh

Possible first contributions include:

- one lesson from a course or project and how you applied it;
- a short event reflection with a specific takeaway;
- a resource plus an explanation of who may find it useful;
- a behind-the-scenes look at a process without exposing confidential work;
- a focused question that shows the research you already completed.

Avoid presenting other people's ideas as your own, inventing results, exposing private information, or manufacturing a personal story for engagement.

## Maintain the system

Use two short sessions each week rather than an unsustainable daily target. One can focus on reading and contributing; the other on relationships, messages, and follow-up.

Once a month, test your contact and portfolio links, review pending messages, and check whether your headline still matches your direction. Once a quarter, update evidence, remove outdated claims, and review visibility and job preferences.

> A useful LinkedIn presence is consistent with your real work. The goal is not constant performance—it is making relevant evidence and professional generosity easier to find.
